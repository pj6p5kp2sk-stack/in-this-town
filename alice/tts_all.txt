In this town, if your visa runs out, a red laser falls from the sky and kills you. Arisu didn't know that yet. He was just a lazy gamer, standing in the middle of Shibuya with his two best friends, Karube and Chota. Then, for a few seconds, the whole city went dark. When the lights came back on, every single person in Tokyo was gone. No cars. No phones. No people. That night, a glowing screen invited them to play a game. Every game had a playing card. Spades were physical. Clubs were teamwork. Diamonds were brains. And hearts were the worst, because hearts play with your feelings. Win, and you get a visa, a few more days to live. Lose, and the laser finds you. Arisu was good at games, so for a while, they kept winning. Then they walked into a dark garden, and the card on the screen was a heart. The game was hide and seek. And then they heard the last rule. Only one of you can survive. Arisu looked at his friends. His friends looked at him. Karube and Chota gave their lives, so Arisu could live. Alone and broken, Arisu wanted to give up. Until someone told him about a place where players had a plan to go home. The Beach. In this town, the safest place to live is a hotel called the Beach. At least, that's what everyone thinks. Arisu arrived with Usagi, a mountain climber who had lost everything too. The Beach was full of pool parties, loud music and hundreds of players in swimsuits, all following one rule. Give every card you win to the Hatter. The Hatter promised that if they collected the whole deck, from ace to king, they could all go home. But the Beach was hiding things. Chishiya, a quiet genius in a white hoodie, was planning to steal the cards. A group with guns wanted to take control. And one night, a girl was found dead in her room. Suddenly, the whole hotel became a game. Find the killer before time runs out, or everyone dies. Friends turned on friends, guns came out, and the Beach burned. Arisu solved the game, but the dream of going home was gone. Then, one by one, every number card in Tokyo was cleared. Giant balloons rose over the city, carrying new cards. Jacks, queens and kings. That was the real twist. Collecting the cards was never the way out. It was only the first level. The face cards were not players. They were the people who live in the borderland and make the rules. And to go home, Arisu would have to beat every single one of them. In this town, the last game isn't played with guns. It's played with a cup of tea. But first, Arisu had to survive the face cards. The King of Spades walked through the streets with a giant gun, hunting every player he could find. The Jack of Hearts locked people in a prison, where your life depended on a collar you couldn't see and the lies of everyone around you. The King of Clubs made teams fight for points until only one team was left. Usagi almost gave up. Arisu almost lost his mind. But they kept going, together. At the very end, only one card was left. The Queen of Hearts. Her name was Mira, and her game was croquet. Just croquet, and tea. But while they played, Mira told Arisu a terrible story. None of this was real. There was no borderland. He was making it all up in his head. Arisu started to believe her. Then he remembered Karube, Chota, and everyone who died to keep him alive, and he refused to quit. He won. And then, he opened his eyes in a hospital bed. A meteor had crashed into Shibuya, and everyone in the borderland had been stuck between life and death. Arisu and Usagi survived, and they finally met in the real world. But somewhere, on a table full of cards, one card was still left. The Joker.
